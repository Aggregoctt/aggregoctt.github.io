Keep in mind that there is no Founder's Edition of the 5070 Ti. This model is only available as an aftermarket card. A base model GeForce RTX 5070 Ti card was supposed to retail for $749.99 when it was first released in February earlier this year. This hadn't been the case with all of the price gouging and the cost of an RTX 5070 Ti went up to as high as $1,000, but supply has finally caught up with demand. There are several different RTX 5070 Ti editions that offer better cooling, flashier colors, or slight overclocking out of the box, but you'll get 95% of the performance with the base model. Since Nvidia GPU prices are already high, I recommend sticking with the least expensive edition you can find.

The RTX 5070 Ti GPU Has Excellent 4K Gaming Performance

Of all the Blackwell cards released thus far, the RTX 5070 Ti offers the best bang for your buck, especially when pitted against the previous generation GPUs. It performs neck-and-neck with the RTX 4080 Super and already marginalizes the RTX 5080, which is only about 10%-15% faster but costs 33% more. Like all Blackwell cards, the RTX 5070 Ti supports DLSS 4 with multi-grame generation. This GPU is capable of high framerates in nearly all games, even at 4K resolution with ray tracing enabled. Heck, even if you plan on using this card for AI, the RTX 5070 Ti might be the better value than the RTX 5080 since both are equipped with the same 16GB of GDDR7 VRAM.
