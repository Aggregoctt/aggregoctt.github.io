A VTuber is begging Twitch for help after receiving “dozens” of offline Prime subscriptions to her channel every five minutes, claiming the platform found no issue with the situation.

On December 28, 2025, VTuber ‘FoxxyLilly’ shared a screenshot showing notifications for numerous offline Prime subscriptions to her channel over the course of three hours.

The streamer urged Twitch to take action in a post on X, speculating that it was “100% some sort of weird scheme to harm me” and an attempt to get her channel banned for fraudulent activity.

She claimed that she’d received a response from the streaming website, claiming they “told me they can’t do anything.”

Several other broadcasters revealed that a similar issue had happened to them, with many speculating that a bad actor possibly paid for the subscription bots as a way of harassing her and potentially getting her suspended.

VTubers report mass influx of suspicious Prime subs

FoxxyLilly updated her fans and fellow streamers in another post on December 31, where she continued to plead with Twitch to look into the constant Prime subscriptions to her channel that hadn’t stopped over the last three days.

“After reaching out to Twitch multiple times, all I got from them was that they can’t do anything or they’re simply saying that no actions are required here (I feel like they didn’t even read everything lol just speculation though),” she wrote.

“As of now, the botting [has] started and stopped multiple times, and as of right now making the post, it is STILL ON GOING… I contacted twitch support multiple times via their ticket system and their live chat but as mentioned above, they’re not getting their asses up at all.”

Concerned viewers urged the streamer to disconnect her account before getting paid from the subscriptions to avoid chargebacks, while others told her to contact Amazon directly, as the subscriptions were made using accounts with Prime.

For now, FoxxyLilly has not given any further updates on her situation as other streamers also sound off on the issue, hoping that the platform will take action once the holidays are officially over.
