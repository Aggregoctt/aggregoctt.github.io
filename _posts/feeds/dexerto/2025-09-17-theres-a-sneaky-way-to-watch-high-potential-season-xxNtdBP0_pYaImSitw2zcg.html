Kaitlin Olson is back in High Potential Season 2, and if you want to keep up with Morgan’s investigations, there’s one way you can stream it for free.

The crime drama series premiered in September last year, and just like The Pitt (and very much unlike Severance and Stranger Things), its second season has been turned around incredibly quickly. It was renewed in January this year, and the first episode dropped this week – that’s a mere nine months.

It follows Morgan Gillory (Olson, best known for her role in It’s Always Sunny in Philadelphia), a cleaner for the LAPD who happens to have an IQ of 160, making her significantly smarter than pretty much everyone around her. When she solves a complex case, she’s recruited as a civilian consultant.

With Season 2 picking up almost immediately after the end of the first season and Morgan still being targeted by the Game Master, it’s the perfect time to jump into High Potential.

How to watch High Potential Season 2 for free

New episodes of High Potential Season 2 drop on Hulu the day after they air on ABC, which means you can watch the series (or at least some of it) with the help of a free trial.

It’s simple, as long as you don’t accidentally select one of the bundles you’ll see on the website. Hulu wants you to pick a package that includes Disney+, ESPN, and/or HBO Max – but you don’t need to commit to one of those deals. Plus, they won’t give you a 30-day free trial.

Here’s what you need to do: go to this page and scroll to the bottom. You should see a small section that says, “Looking for something else?” Click the link that says, “Learn about all our plans.”

Scroll down until you find Hulu’s standalone plans, and select Hulu (No Ads). You’ll need to sign up for an account if you don’t have one already. Once that’s done, you’ll need to add your billing info.

You won’t be charged for 30 days – but if you forget to cancel your subscription within the free trial period, you’ll automatically be charged for a whole month. Set a reminder on your calendar so you don’t forget – or keep your subscription if you like what Hulu has to offer!
