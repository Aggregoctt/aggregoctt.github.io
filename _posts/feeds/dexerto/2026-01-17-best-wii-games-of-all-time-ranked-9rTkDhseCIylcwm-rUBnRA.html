When Nintendo launched the Wii in 2006, it didn’t try to outmuscle its rivals on power — it changed how people played instead. Motion controls, an accessible design philosophy, and a focus on shared experiences turned gaming into something the whole room could join, not just the person holding the controller.

That shift paid off. Wii Sports became a cultural phenomenon overnight, while Mario Kart Wii and Super Smash Bros. Brawl turned living rooms into instant party arenas. At the same time, Nintendo’s core franchises thrived in ambitious new ways. Super Mario Galaxy and Galaxy 2 redefined 3D platforming, The Legend of Zelda: Twilight Princess and Skyward Sword delivered epic adventures, and Metroid Prime 3 and Xenoblade Chronicles pushed scale and immersion beyond expectations for the hardware.

It was also a console where creative risks flourished. Okami, No More Heroes, MadWorld, and World of Goo showed that unconventional ideas could find a massive audience. Whether it was family-friendly fun or deep single-player epics, the Wii’s library proved that innovation and imagination could outsell raw power.

With that in mind, here’s our ranking of the best Wii games.
