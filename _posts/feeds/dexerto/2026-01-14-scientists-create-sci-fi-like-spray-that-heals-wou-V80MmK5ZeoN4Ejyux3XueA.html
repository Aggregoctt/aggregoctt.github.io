Scientists have developed a spray-on powder that can seal serious wounds in around a second, stopping bleeding far faster than traditional dressings.

The powder was created by researchers at the Korea Advanced Institute of Science and Technology (via SciTechDaily), who designed it to work on deep or irregular injuries where bandages and patches can struggle.

When sprayed onto a bleeding wound, the powder reacts with elements in the blood and rapidly turns into a gel, forming a barrier that helps halt bleeding almost immediately.

How the spray-on powder works

Rather than soaking up blood like conventional dressings, the powder actively seals the wound. It is made from biocompatible materials that react with calcium in the blood, causing the powder to solidify into a gel-like layer in about one second.

In testing, the powder was also shown to be highly absorbent, forming a three-dimensional structure that allowed it to soak up roughly 725% of its weight, useful in stabilizing a bleed before the gel formed.

The researchers say the spray format allows it to be applied quickly and easily, even to complex wounds, which could make it useful in emergency situations where speed is critical. The powder is also designed to remain stable during storage, meaning it can be kept ready for use when needed.

The technology is still in the research stage and has not yet been approved for medical use in people.
