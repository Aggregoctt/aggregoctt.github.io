Roblox’s Goalbound pits players against each other in an intense 5v5 football-based match, using tactics, abilities, and skill to dominate and score goals. Though it’s all made easier with the right Style and Ego.

Styles in Goalbound are essentially the characters you use in your match. They’re inspired by anime fighters and all have their own stills, abilities, strengths, and weaknesses. As such, some are much better than others.

Egos are the same, offering separate boosts to your Styles. They can increase movement, stamina, power, and protect shots from hitting the back of the net. However, they too aren’t equal in power, so you’ll need to master both Styles and Egos in order to thrive in Goalbound.

Goalbound Styles tier list

Our tiers explained

  • S: The best
  • A: Very good
  • B: There might be situations where they can perform well
  • C: Best to avoid unless you find yourself drawn to them

S-Tier Styles

Shidou

Shidou is a fantastic Style in Goalbound and is guaranteed to glide across the pitch with their insane speed and power. Mixing Dragon Drive after super sprint is the best way to go, making Shidou fast, nimble, and extremely powerful. They may be Mythic, but if you’re lucky enough to pull them, then they’re perfect.

Sae

Sae is a wonderful passer in Goalbound, working best when placed in the middle of the field and focusing primarily on the assist. Their power and speed allow them to get the ball wherever they need, and their passing skills are second to none thanks to Perfect Pass. Just like Shidou, Sae is a Mythic, so it won’t be easy to get hold of them.

Rin

Rin is an extremely powerful offensive Style, using great mobility and defense anklebreak to completely dominate the enemy. Your shots are powerful thanks to Curve Shot, and you’re almost guaranteed to leave the other team in shambles. Play offensively, and you’ll be dominating the battlefield if you’re lucky enough to pull this Mythic.

A-Tier Styles

Barou

Barou is a fantastic Style to get hold of, especially if you want simple yet effective abilities. He’s easy to master, extremely mobile, and has plenty of shooting power to boot. Combine that with some stellar dribbling thanks to Lightning Dribble, and you have a great all-rounder that’s easy to master. He might not have too much high-skill play potential, but if you want an easy game, Barou is your man.

Nagi

Nagi has a powerful ability to stop any shot in the game, thanks to their Trap move. Combine that with the volley shots, dribbles, and defense capabilities, and you have easily the best goalkeeper or defense Style in Goalbound. Given that, he’s not the best at offense, so if you’re okay keeping back, that’s where Nagi will shine.

Gagamaru

Gagamaru is an extremely versatile Style in Goalbound, offering powerful abilities for both defense and offense. He’s a wonderful support and even better goalkeeper thanks to Bear Deflect. However, he can also thrive in offense due to his Shachihoko and Awakening shot. The only issue is his mobility, which is lacking in comparison to the S-tier Styles. Nevertheless, master him, and you’ll thrive.

Bachira

Bachira isn’t an easy Style to use in Goalbound. There are tons of combos, dribble tactics, rainbow flicks, unpredictable shots, and power to master in order to get the best out of them. If you manage to perfect them, then they’re easily S-tier, but we’ve kept them in A due to how long it’ll take to perfect, and how tough it is to really find that sweet spot.

B-Tier Styles

Chigiri

Despite sitting in B-tier, Chigiri has some of the best off-ball mobility in Goalbound. However, that’s pretty much all they have. He’s unpredictable at close range and has this annoying injury bar that prevents you from spamming his moves, making him pretty useless in any intense matches.

Kunigami

Kunigami is a pretty powerful Rare, and is easy to get hold of in comparison to the other powerful Styles. However, his mobility and how long it takes to master that are the only reasons why he’s sitting in B-tier. It’s so hard to perfect, and if you have anymore than two people on you, you’re going to get overwhelmed with your counters. Sure, he’s flexible and has the best close-range shot, but you need to learn him first.

C-Tier Styles

Isagi

Isagi may be the most common Style in Goalbound, but he’s by far the worst. He’s predictable, slow, and struggles to keep up with the higher tiers. Sure, he’s got good mobility and is a great beginner’s option, but as soon as you get more skilled, leave Isagi behind.

So, those are all the best Styles. Next, you’ll need to know the best Egos to truly thrive in Goalbound.

Ego tier list

Our tiers explained

  • S: The best
  • A: Very good
  • B: There might be situations where these can perform well
  • C: Best to avoid unless you find yourself drawn to them

S-Tier Egos

Genius

Genius is a fantastic Ego offering great kick power and stamina management. It offers a powerful reset ability and thrives when you play aggressively. This is purely for an offensive Style or player, so utilize it well, and you’ll see why it’s in S-tier.

A-Tier Egos

Dribbler

Similar to Genius, you’ll want to use Dribbler if you’re an offensive player who’s particularly aggressive on the battlefield. It offers shortened cooldowns, reduced stamina, improved speed, and is just perfect for getting where you need to quickly. Of course, you’ll want to use it for dribble-oriented Styles like Shidou.

Iron Wall

Iron Wall is a great Ego in Goalbound, becoming the only real viable option for goalkeepers. Sure, it offers good defense, but it also expands the diving hitbox, which will help you save more than one goal. If you’re playing as a goalie, this is the Ego you need.

King

King has the highest power boost in Goalbound, offering plenty of bonuses when tackling, kicking, and dominating the battlefield. Sure, it does allow you to tackle your teammates, but if you coordinate, you’ll be able to thrive with this Ego.

Hero

Hero is a great Ego. It thrives when you’re getting surrounded by increasing your shot power and offers walk speed and kick enhancements to help you dominate. If you’re a midfielder, you’ll want to use the Hero Ego.

B-Tier Egos

Speedster

Speedster does what it says on the tin, it increases your speed and kick. However, where it lets itself down is the fact that it doesn’t increase your stamina. As such, you can move fast and kick hard at the cost of high stamina, meaning it doesn’t last long. Sure, it’s useful, but it’s just not as good as it sounds.

Prodigy

Prodigy is a pretty well-balanced Ego in Goalbound, offering boosts in your kick, speed, and stamina. Interestingly, it offers some of the best stamina boosts in the game. But that’s where its perks end. Outside of that, it has no passive or active ability, meaning you’re extremely limited and get no real boosts aside from the ones you can activate.

C-Tier Egos

Egoist

Egoist just fails to live up to any of its counterparts, unfortunately. Sure, it’s got a good boost to walk speed and stamina, but anything it can do, other Egos can do so much better. It’s ideal for offense, but so is Genius, which offers extra benefits too.
