TikTok has signed a deal, expected to close on January 22, 2026, that sees the American arm of the company spun off under a new entity majority controlled by U.S. investors, safeguarding the app’s future in the nation.

Back in April, 2024, then-President Joe Biden signed a bill that required TikTok’s Chinese parent company, ByteDance, to sell a majority of its shares or face a ban in the U.S. The law banning TikTok in the US took effect on January 19, 2025, briefly blocking the app, however, President Donald Trump quickly overturned the ruling and has continuously extended the deadline throughout the year.

Now, after a fourth delay from Trump, TikTok has inked a deal. As first reported by Axios, its U.S. assets are to be spun off into a new entity that will be majority-owned by U.S. investors.

Oracle, Silver Lake, and MGX will jointly control a 50% stake in the new entity. The rest will consist of 30.1% from affiliates of ByteDance investors, and 19.9% retained by ByteDance itself. This ensures, at least for the immediate future, TikTok will continue operating in the U.S.

TikTok avoids U.S. ban with new deal

The deal isn’t finalized just yet, with Chinese government approval also expected, though it is reportedly set to close on January 22, according to an internal company memo first circulated by Axios.

TikTok’s U.S. entity will boast a board of seven directors, with the majority being American. Furthermore, U.S. user data will be stored in the U.S. moving forward.

On the user’s end, Americans may notice changes to the content being fed on their feed. TikTok is reportedly set to ‘retrain’ its algorithm as part of the deal, “[ensuring] the content feed is free from outside manipulation.”

This mostly U.S. collective will also be responsible for U.S. content moderation moving forward.
